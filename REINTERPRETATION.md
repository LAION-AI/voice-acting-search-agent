# Reinterpreting existing recordings at scale — a procedural recipe guide

**Scenario.** You have a large corpus of speech clips (e.g. ~1M DramaBox outputs). Each clip already
carries: **EmoNet-40** emotion scores (Empathic-Insight-Voice), **VoiceNet-57** dimension scores,
**genuineness** + **vocal-burst-blend**, a **transcript**, and **inline vocal-burst detections**. You want
to regenerate each clip in a *different* delivery ("reinterpretation") with `MOSS-VA-v2`
(`laion/moss-tts-local-transformer-4.55b-voice-acting-v2`) — louder, sadder, more menacing, more explicit,
whatever the target is — **without paying an LLM call per clip**.

This page gives a **deterministic procedural rule**: input scores → (LoRA set + merge scales + prompt +
sampling), grounded in our experiments. It also answers the engineering question: *can I hot-swap LoRAs fast
enough to give each batch its own recipe at 1M scale?* (Short answer: yes, if you **bucket by recipe** —
§4.)

Everything here is either **[validated]** (measured in an experiment, cited) or **[recommended]**
(extrapolated from those experiments — flagged as such). It distills:
[emotion manual](https://laion-ai.github.io/moss-voiceacting-manual/) ·
[VoiceNet dimension manual](https://laion-ai.github.io/moss-voicenet-manual/) ·
[Emotion×Voice conditions study](https://laion-ai.github.io/emotion-voice-conditions/) ·
[DramaBox reinterpret pages](https://projects.laion.ai/Voice-Acting-Pipeline/dramabox_reinterpret.html) ·
this repo's `context/system_context.md`, `docs/experience-digest.md`, `MODEL_COMPARISON.md`.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph version

Read the clip's scores. Decide a **target** (emotion and/or VoiceNet deltas). Map the target-emotion's
**z-score → intensity tier → emotion-LoRA λ** (from the evolved per-emotion table, §1). Turn each **desired
VoiceNet delta → a `vn_<DIM>_high/low` dose** (from the dose-response tables, §2). If the target is
high-energy, **add the stabilizers `vn_ARSH_high@0.5 + vn_BRGT_high@0.5`** to protect intelligibility (§2.3).
Cap the *total* added scale so genuineness survives (balanced 0.8–1.1 beats extremes). Build the **prompt**
from a procedural caption of the *target* delivery + a condition cue + the original transcript with its burst
tags (§3). Pick **sampling** by whether you condition on the original as reference (§3.4). Optionally
**SIDON-finalize** (§2.4). Then **bucket the corpus by the discrete recipe and process one bucket per batch**
so LoRA swaps are near-free (§4).

## 1. Emotion → LoRA λ (the intensity dial)

**Per-emotion evolved λ.** Each of the 40 emotion LoRAs (`TTS-AGI/moss-emotion-loras-v3`) has an evolved
"full-intensity" λ (from the [40-emotion evolution](https://laion-ai.github.io/moss-emotion-evolution-all/),
hall-of-fame genomes). λ ranges **0.5–2.0**; e.g. Anger 2.0, Concentration 2.0, Confusion 2.0, Amusement 1.5,
Contempt 1.5, Disgust 1.5, Distress 1.5, Contentment 1.0, Bitterness 1.0, Affection 0.75, Awe 0.5,
Disappointment 0.5. **The full table is in `context/system_context.md` (regenerated by `build_context.py`) and
`/tmp/evo_all/<Emotion>.json` → `hof[0].genome.lam`.** [validated]

**Map the input clip's target-emotion z-score to an intensity tier → λ:**

| you want the emotion... | tier | emotion-LoRA λ | prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| barely present / a hint | subtle | **0** (prompt only) | mild descriptor + light cue |
| clearly present, controlled | moderate | **0.5** (fixed) | moderate descriptor + cue |
| strong, full delivery | intense | **evolved λ, capped ≤ 1.5** for speech / **≤ 1.9** if intelligibility may suffer | intense descriptor + evolved cue |

- **[validated] Cap intensity at ~1.5 for intelligible speech.** In the
  [Emotion×Voice study](https://laion-ai.github.io/emotion-voice-conditions/), intense conditions pushed λ to
  1.9 and produced the *most* felt emotion (pooled emo 0.47) but also the most speaker-similarity failures and
  the worst genuineness — **raw intensity is what pulls a cloned voice off-timbre, not the emotion itself.**
- **[validated] Some emotions are better prompt-only.** The [emotion manual](https://laion-ai.github.io/moss-voiceacting-manual/)
  flags emotions where the LoRA *lowers* reward (it raises the target but craters genu/blend/WER more than a
  well-worded prompt does). For those, use **λ=0** and steer with the prompt. Check the per-emotion
  "best strategy (with/without LoRA)" field before merging.
- **[validated] Prompt-only + moderate always beats intense on genuineness/blend.** Condition **D**
  (moderate-contained) had the best blend and best-preserved voice; condition **C** (intense-contained) was
  the *least* natural of all four.

**Multiple/mixed emotions:** the input often has two co-active emotions. Merge **both** LoRAs but scale the
secondary down (~0.5× its tier). Beware antagonistic pairs — the manual's top-3 ± correlations show which
emotions co-move (safe to stack) vs. oppose (don't). Total emotion-scale should still respect the §2.5 cap.

## 2. VoiceNet deltas → dimension-LoRA doses (the timbre/prosody dials)

For each VoiceNet dimension you want to change, compute **Δ = target − measured** and turn it into a dose using
the [VoiceNet manual](https://laion-ai.github.io/moss-voicenet-manual/) dose-response tables
(`/tmp/vn_manual/entries.json`). 114 LoRAs = 57 dims × {high, low} in `laion/moss-voicenet-dimension-loras`.

### 2.1 The dose curve [validated]
- **`__high` is a strong, clean, roughly monotonic knob.** Each 25→125% step pushes the target further; best
  effect at **0.75–1.25**. Energy/prosody dims are the cleanest: Pitch Range Δ+0.76, Arousal +0.73,
  Ranting/Angry +0.75, Dynamic Arc +0.63, Register +0.61 — all monotonic.
- **`__low` is a weak knob.** Pulling a dimension *down* barely moves the target (Arousal-low only −0.09,
  Attack-low −0.02, Valence-low −0.01) and its best dose sits at **1.25** (needs max dose to do anything).
  → Prefer expressing a target as a `__high` on the *opposite-sense* dimension when one exists.

### 2.2 The universal cost [validated]
Almost every `__high` merge **cuts vocal-burst blend by −0.40 to −0.46** (worst: Authoritative −0.46,
Metallic −0.44, Arousal −0.43). **Genuineness, by contrast, is robust** — the worst genu hit anywhere is only
−0.14. So: merges buy their effect by sacrificing *burst-blend*, while leaving naturalness largely intact.
Budget for the blend drop; if blend matters for the scene (e.g. it has inline laughs/sighs), keep doses ≤1.0.

### 2.3 Stabilizers for high-energy targets [validated — the key discovery]
When the target is high-arousal / loud / intense, **add `vn_ARSH_high@~0.5` + `vn_BRGT_high@~0.5`** to the
merge. In the model comparison the 31B agent *discovered on its own* that these two adapters act as
**intelligibility stabilizers** — they let you push energy high while keeping **WER near zero**
(`MODEL_COMPARISON.md`). Independently reproduced by the gpt-5.6-luna arm (arousal 0.48→0.78 at **WER 0.000**
with `vn_DARC_high@0.85 + vn_ARSH_high@0.5`). Treat this as the default "energy-without-garble" pair.

### 2.4 SIDON finalize (optional) [validated]
Running the winning take through `sidon_enhance` (restoration) recovers a little of what heavy merging
costs: **GENU +0.03, BLEND +0.04, QUALITY +0.04** on faithful takes. But it only stays faithful on ~half of
heavy-merge takes, so **keep the envelope-fidelity guard on** (corr > 0.85 vs. input) and use it as a
*finalize* step, never as a score booster.

### 2.5 The genuineness/WER budget (cap total scale) [validated]
- **Balanced multi-merges (each scale 0.8–1.1) beat single-LoRA extremes.** In the D benchmark, the balanced
  genome was the *only* one to keep BLEND above baseline; single big doses broke it.
- **Always multiply the selection reward by (1 − WER)** when choosing among candidates — this is what stops
  the "overacted, unintelligible" failure mode. (Pre-WER runs produced a storyteller winner that scored high
  but was genuinely unintelligible.) For deliberately non-speech targets (screams), drop the WER term.
- Rule of thumb: **total added scale ≲ 2.5–3.0** across all merged adapters (emotion + VoiceNet), unless the
  scene is meant to be extreme. [recommended]

### 2.6 Aesthetics [recommended, under active test]
For scenes that should be *pleasant to listen to* (storytelling, warm/positive delivery), add a
**+0.5·norm(ESTH)** term to the selection reward (VoiceNet Aesthetics, code `ESTH`, already in the 57). An
EIV-Plus "content-enjoyment" head is being wired as `ENJOY` where available. This is the current
gpt-5.6-luna experiment — results will land in `MODEL_COMPARISON.md`.

## 3. Prompt construction

MOSS-VA-v2 takes `GENERAL:` (a description of the voice + delivery) and `SCRIPT:` (a delivery cue + the text).

### 3.1 GENERAL = a procedural caption of the *target* delivery
Reuse the **procedural-voice-captions** phrasing
([repo](https://github.com/LAION-AI/procedural-voice-captions), `caption(preds, baseline)`), but feed it the
**target** score vector (measured + your intended deltas), not the input's. This yields a natural-language
description ("a warm, slightly breathy voice, decelerating in pace…") that matches the LoRA merge you built —
prompt and merge then reinforce instead of fighting.

### 3.2 SCRIPT cue = intensity × containment
Map the intensity tier (§1) and whether the feeling is *let out* vs *held back* to the A/B/C/D cue templates
from the [Emotion×Voice study](https://laion-ai.github.io/emotion-voice-conditions/):

| condition | cue template |
|---|---|
| **A** intense, free | `(letting it out fully, unrestrained)` |
| **B** moderate, free | `(<adj>, <emotion> showing lightly)` |
| **C** intense, contained | `(struggling to suppress it, jaw tight, it breaks through in flashes)` |
| **D** moderate, contained | `(quietly holding it in, only a trace showing)` |

For **edge cases / vocal bursts**, use the evolved cue from `/tmp/edge_evo/*.json` (e.g. fear-scream
`(terrified, screaming, voice cracking with panic)`, Fear-LoRA λ 0.75, temp 1.6, plus a `<screaming>` tag;
sad-cry `(sobbing, voice trembling and breaking)`, Sadness λ 0.75).

### 3.3 Text = original transcript, bursts preserved
Keep the original words. **Preserve the inline vocal-burst tags** from the input's burst detections
(`<laugh>`, `<sigh>`, `[pause 0.4s]`, …) — they are part of the performance and MOSS renders them. Use the
detected burst *type* to bias the emotion choice if the transcript is ambiguous.

### 3.4 Sampling [validated]
- **With reference conditioning** (original clip as reference, for voice preservation): **temp 0.8–0.9,
  top_p 0.9, top_k 40**. Lower temperature keeps the clone stable.
- **No reference** (free regeneration of a new voice): **temp 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 25**.
- **Bursts need heat**: for real non-speech bursts, temp ≥ 1.5 (else the LoRA suppresses the burst).

### 3.5 Duration [recommended]
Aim for scene takes around the length of the source (or ~8–12 s for standalone scenes): `max_new_frames ≈
125` (≈10 s at 12.5 Hz). Add a soft selection preference for the intended duration band so cohorts don't
collapse to 2-second fragments.

## 4. Reference conditioning: when to clone vs. regenerate [validated]

- **Condition on the original clip** when you want to *keep the same speaker* and only change the delivery.
  Enforce a **speaker-similarity floor** (ECAPA ≥ 0.50) when selecting among candidates — reject takes that
  drifted to a different voice. Note the trade-off: the more intensity you add, the more candidates fall below
  the floor (intense conditions carried ~90% of all below-floor cells in the study), so **contain the
  intensity if voice preservation matters more than raw affect.**
- **Regenerate freely (no reference)** when the scene calls for a *different* voice (e.g. a character
  transformation). Then quiet/soft extremes actually clone/transfer *better* than loud ones — screams and
  shouting leave the least speaker identity to work with.

## 5. Hot-swapping LoRAs at 1M scale — the engineering answer

**The concern:** if every clip could want a different LoRA combo, do we pay a cold model/adapter load
(≈0.5–1 min) per batch? **Answer: no — as long as adapters are preloaded and you bucket by recipe.**

### 5.1 Swap speed [validated in our own code]
- **PEFT multi-adapter (what the search agent + all our sweep scripts use):** preload N adapters once
  (`PeftModel.load_adapter` per adapter), then switching the *active* set is just
  `set_adapter([...])` + rescaling `m.scaling[name] = base·λ` — **microseconds to milliseconds**, no I/O. Our
  VoiceNet sweep merges/re-scales adapters thousands of times this way with no reload.
- **Cold load** of an adapter not yet in memory: **~1–3 s** (read a ~275 MB rank-32 safetensors + attach). Do
  this once, up front, not per batch.
- **VRAM budget:** a rank-32 adapter ≈ **275 MB**. On an 80 GB A100 next to the 4.55B model + codec + scorers
  (~46 GB), you have room for a **working set of ~20–40 preloaded adapters** — enough to cover the whole
  recipe vocabulary for a corpus at once.
- **vLLM multi-LoRA:** per-request LoRA selection from a *preloaded* pool is ms-level; a LoRA not in the pool
  is a cold load. (vLLM-Omni support for the MOSS codec path is **unverified** — validate before relying on
  it; the PEFT path above is proven for us.)

### 5.2 The scaling pattern: **bucket by recipe, not by clip** [recommended]
The procedural rule (§1–§3) is **deterministic and discretised** — intensity tiers, dose steps (0/0.5/0.75/
1.0/1.25), a finite emotion set. So a million clips map to a **finite set of distinct recipes** (realistically
hundreds, not millions). Therefore:

```
1. Run the procedural rule over the whole corpus (pure CPU, from the precomputed scores) → assign each
   clip a recipe_id = hash(sorted LoRA-set + scales + cue-template + sampling-preset).
2. Group clips by recipe_id.
3. Preload the union of all adapters referenced (one-time cold load; fits in the working set, else page
   the least-used).
4. Process one recipe bucket at a time: set_adapter+scale ONCE per bucket (ms), then stream that bucket's
   clips through generation as large batches. Swap cost per bucket ≈ 0; swaps ≈ number of recipes, not
   number of clips.
```

This turns "1M adapter swaps" into "~hundreds of ms-level swaps" and keeps the GPU saturated on generation.
No LLM call anywhere in the loop — the recipe comes from the procedural rule, and candidate selection uses the
scorers + the `(1−WER)` reward.

### 5.3 Worked example
Input clip scores (abridged): dominant EmoNet = **Sadness z+1.8**; VoiceNet **Arousal 0.7 (high), Warmth 0.2
(low)**; genuineness 0.6; transcript `"I told you it would end like this. <sigh>"`. Target: *make it a
restrained, sorrowful, warm delivery of the same line, same speaker.*

- Emotion: Sadness, intensity **moderate-contained (D)** → Sadness-LoRA **λ 0.5** (keep the voice), cue
  `(quietly holding it in, only a trace showing)`.
- VoiceNet deltas: want Warmth **up** → `vn_WARM_high@0.75`; want Arousal **down** → don't use the weak
  Arousal-low; instead let the contained cue + lower temp carry it. No stabilizers (low-energy target).
- GENERAL: procedural caption of the target vector → *"a warm, soft, slightly breathy voice, decelerating in
  pace, gentle and restrained."*
- Text: `"I told you it would end like this. <sigh>"` (burst preserved).
- Reference: **yes** (same speaker) → sampling temp 0.85 / top_p 0.9 / top_k 40; spk-sim floor 0.5.
- Recipe_id groups this with every other "Sadness-moderate-D + WARM_high@0.75" clip → one bucket, one swap.

## 6. Known limits (don't burn compute where the engine can't deliver) [validated]

From the 8-task edge-case swarm, some targets are **currently engine-limited** — flag them early instead of
spending generations:

- **Solvable well:** hoarse voice, calm-narration-under-tension, fading/dying voice, restrained sorrow, warm
  positive delivery, single short screams.
- **Hard / engine-limited:** tongue-clicks *mid-speech*, genuine crying *within a sentence* (vs. a sustained
  sob), effort-speech while physically exerting (climbing), abrupt jump-scare bursts, sustained rhythmic
  non-speech. For these the supervisor honestly scored 1–2 even as fitness climbed — the ceiling is the TTS
  model, not the search.
